How each report was placed

  1. Repeats out first. The ticket export comes in overlapping pages, so one id can appear twice. One id, one report.
  2. One-day moves. The desk already read some reports as a one-day move (desk-notes.json): Tuesday shown as Monday, 12 May as 11 May, one meeting on two days for Lisbon and Toronto. Day and month are not swapped there, so the parser cannot cause it. The incident table files them under parser by tenant; here they are their own problem.
  3. The rest by tenant, not by intake tag. Every tenant on file writes the day first. A report that came through the booking form of a booking-v3 tenant is hint: that form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. Every other report is parser. The intake tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser and disagrees often.

Why it matters

  • parser is the fix already queued. Ship it.
  • hint survives that fix: the form tells people to type month first and then reads the date day first. Change the hint on booking-v3 to the tenant’s order.
  • shift survives both: the dates are read right and still land a day early. Look at time zones and DST where an invite’s date is turned into a timestamp.

Lessons

  • Matching on subject merged three causes into one ticket (decision 0001). A count of reports says nothing about how many problems they hold.
  • The desk saw the one-day moves and wrote them down, then filed them where the tenant pointed. A note that disagrees with the cause is a new problem, not a footnote.
